# Interview Guide

**Study:** Making the City Legible: A Human-Centred Geodatabase for Urban Planning and Local Government in Bangladesh

**Case:** Rajshahi City Corporation (RCC)

**Purpose:** To understand how spatial information is created, stored, shared, verified, and used in RCC’s daily work, and to identify the requirements, risks, and governance conditions for a municipal geodatabase.

**Audience for the article:** urban planners, policymakers, academics, and informed general users.

**Interview length:** 45–60 minutes.

**Format:** semi-structured, key-informant interview. The questions below are a guide, not a script. Follow the participant’s experience and ask for concrete examples.

---

## 1. What the interview is trying to learn

The interviews should produce evidence for five research questions:

| RQ | Focus |
|---|---|
| **RQ1** | Global lessons on municipal GIS, benefits, complexities, and governance |
| **RQ2** | Data model, architecture, and automation needs for a paper/Excel starting point |
| **RQ3** | Governance, capacity, and financing arrangements |
| **RQ4** | Public value in planning, revenue, service delivery, and accountability |
| **RQ5** | Implementation roadmap and replication |

The interview is not a survey. It is a structured conversation about **how work is actually done**.

---

## 2. Who to interview

Target 12–15 participants. A minimum viable sample is 10.

| Code | Participant type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P01 | CEO / Additional CEO | Mandate, budget, coordination, political support |
| P02 | Head of ICT / GIS focal point | Systems, data, standards, security |
| P03 | Revenue officer / holding-tax supervisor | Property, address, tax, valuation |
| P04 | Town planning officer | Zoning, land use, permits, plan monitoring |
| P05 | Engineering officer (roads/drains) | Infrastructure, as-built data, maintenance |
| P06 | Conservancy / waste officer | Bins, routes, zones, vehicles, complaints |
| P07 | Parks / health / facilities officer | Public facilities, parks, services |
| P08 | Ward councillor (2 wards) | Local knowledge, citizen requests, accountability |
| P09 | Field inspector / tax assessor | Address ambiguity, field capture, verification |
| P10 | Waste supervisor / field staff | Routes, bins, daily operations |
| P11 | Rajshahi WASA representative | Water/sewer data, mandates, sharing |
| P12 | LGED / RHD / BBS / SoB representative | Inter-agency data, standards, interoperability |
| P13 | Citizen / ward resident | Public information needs, trust, access |
| P14 | Local academic / NGO / civic technologist | External user perspective, data reuse |

Select participants who can speak about **work processes**, not only opinions. A field officer may be more useful than a senior official for understanding data quality.

---

## 3. Before the interview

- Confirm time, place, and duration.
- Send the information sheet and consent form in advance.
- Ask permission to record.
- Bring a notebook and two copies of the consent form.
- Review the participant’s department and role.
- Prepare a blank response template.
- Do not ask for personal tax, owner, or complaint records.
- If the participant offers a document, ask for a blank/redacted version.

---

## 4. Opening script

> Thank you for making time. I am studying how Rajshahi City Corporation uses spatial information — maps, addresses, asset records, registers, drawings — in daily work. I am not evaluating you or your department. I am trying to understand how the work actually happens, what is difficult, and what a better system would need to do.
>
> There are no right or wrong answers. You can skip any question. You can stop at any time. I will not use your name in the article; I will use a code such as P05. If I quote you, I will remove identifying details unless you give permission.
>
> May I record this conversation? The recording will be stored securely and used only for this research.

---

## 5. Core modules and questions

### Module A — Role and daily work

**Q01.** Please describe your role and how long you have been in this department.

**Q02.** Walk me through a typical week. Where do you use maps, addresses, registers, drawings, or spatial information?

**Q03.** Which decisions in your work depend on knowing *where* something is?

Probes:
- Can you give a recent example?
- What happens if the location is wrong?
- Who else needs that information?

---

### Module B — Current data practices

**Q04.** What spatial information do you use most often? (Maps, holding registers, engineering drawings, Excel, GIS, field notes.)

**Q05.** Where is that information kept? Paper, Excel, CAD, GIS, shared drive, personal folder?

**Q06.** Who owns it? Who updates it? Who else can change it?

**Q07.** Think of a recent case — a drainage complaint, a waste collection problem, a holding-tax issue, or a building permit. Walk me through what happened step by step. What information did you need? Where did you find it? Who did you ask?

**Q08.** What documents, forms, or systems did you use in that case?

**Q09.** How did you know the information was correct? What did you do when it was wrong or missing?

**Q10.** How do you share information with other departments or agencies? What works? What fails?

Probes:
- Do you send files, printouts, WhatsApp messages, phone calls?
- Do you use a shared drive?
- Is there a standard template?
- Do you ever receive duplicate or conflicting data?

---

### Module C — Addresses, parcels, buildings, and assets

**Q11.** How are addresses recorded in your work? What makes an address easy or difficult to find?

**Q12.** How do you link a holding or tax record to a building or parcel?

**Q13.** How do you record assets such as drains, manholes, bins, streetlights, parks, or public facilities?

**Q14.** When an asset is repaired, replaced, or newly built, how does that information reach the record?

**Q15.** How do you capture field data? Paper forms, mobile phone, GPS, photos, WhatsApp, Excel?

**Q16.** What happens to as-built drawings and contractor data after a project is completed?

Probes:
- Who receives them?
- Are they georeferenced?
- Are they linked to an asset ID?
- Are they stored in a shared system?

---

### Module D — Data quality and workarounds

**Q17.** What spatial data are most unreliable or incomplete? Can you give an example?

**Q18.** What do you do when the official record does not match what you see on the ground?

**Q19.** How do you resolve duplicate or conflicting records?

**Q20.** What informal workarounds do you and your colleagues use? (Personal Excel sheets, notebooks, WhatsApp groups, memory.)

**Q21.** What would make the data more trustworthy?

---

### Module E — Technology, capacity, and support

**Q22.** What software and devices do you use? What works well? What does not?

**Q23.** Have you received training in GIS, Excel, or data management? What training would help?

**Q24.** Who do you ask when you have a data or software problem?

**Q25.** What would be the biggest challenge for staff in using a shared geodatabase?

Probes:
- Time, workload, skills, language, internet, electricity, fear of monitoring, political pressure.

---

### Module F — Governance, ownership, and coordination

**Q26.** Who should own a city-wide spatial database? Who should maintain it?

**Q27.** How should decisions be made about who can see or publish data?

**Q28.** What data should be public? What should remain internal or restricted?

**Q29.** How should the system handle personal, tax, or complaint data?

**Q30.** What would make departments cooperate — or refuse to cooperate?

Probes:
- Mandate, budget, incentives, leadership, trust, previous failed projects.

---

### Module G — Public value and public information

**Q31.** How do citizens currently get information about services, roads, waste, drains, parks, or facilities?

**Q32.** What do citizens ask for most often?

**Q33.** If RCC published a public map, what would be most useful? What would be risky?

**Q34.** How should the system support Bangla language, low literacy, and low-bandwidth access?

**Q35.** What would make citizens trust the map?

---

### Module H — Implementation priorities and risks

**Q36.** If you could have one map or one data layer tomorrow, what would it be?

**Q37.** What should be done first, second, and third?

**Q38.** What would make this system fail?

**Q39.** What would success look like in two years? How would you measure it?

**Q40.** Is there anything I should have asked but did not?

---

## 6. Tailored question sets

### 6.1 Senior management / policymakers

Add:

- What is the current mandate and budget for GIS/data?
- Which departments would need to co-own the system?
- What political or administrative risks would threaten it?
- What public-value outcomes matter most — revenue, services, planning, transparency?
- How would you justify the investment to the ministry or development partners?
- What would you need to see in the first year to continue funding?

### 6.2 Revenue / holding tax

Add:

- How are holdings identified and numbered?
- How do you match a holding to a building or parcel?
- Where do addresses fail?
- How do you handle new buildings, extensions, and changes of use?
- What data would improve coverage, valuation, or collection?
- How do you handle disputes and corrections?

### 6.3 Town planning

Add:

- What spatial data do you need for plan preparation and development control?
- How do you track permits and development applications?
- How do you monitor whether plans are implemented?
- What data are missing for ward-level planning?
- How should sensitive planning data be published?

### 6.4 Engineering / roads / drains

Add:

- How are roads, drains, manholes, and bridges recorded?
- How do you plan maintenance?
- How do you receive as-built data?
- What condition data do you collect?
- What would help you prioritize repairs?
- How do you link complaints to assets?

### 6.5 Waste / conservancy

Add:

- How are bins, routes, and zones planned?
- How do you know a bin is missing or a route is missed?
- How do you record vehicle and crew data?
- What public information would reduce complaints?
- What data would improve collection efficiency?

### 6.6 GIS / IT

Add:

- What software, servers, and data storage are available?
- What standards, coordinate systems, and metadata are used?
- How are backups, security, and access managed?
- What integration is needed with finance, tax, or engineering systems?
- What would be the safest migration path from Excel/file geodatabase to PostGIS?
- What support and training would be needed?

### 6.7 Field staff

Add:

- What forms or devices do you use in the field?
- How do you record location and photos?
- What errors occur most often?
- What makes a form easy or difficult?
- What would help you work offline?
- How do you report a correction?

### 6.8 Partner agencies

Add:

- What spatial data do you hold that RCC needs?
- What data does RCC hold that you need?
- What are the legal or administrative barriers to sharing?
- What standards or formats do you use?
- Who owns the data?
- What would a data-sharing agreement need to include?

### 6.9 Citizens / public users

Add:

- What information do you need about your ward or street?
- How do you currently find it?
- What language and device do you use?
- What would make a public map trustworthy?
- What should not be public?
- How would you report an error?

## 7. Critical-incident technique

For Q07 and similar questions, use the critical-incident method:

1. Ask for a **specific recent case**.
2. Ask the participant to **walk through the sequence of events**.
3. Ask **who was involved**, **what information was used**, **where it came from**, and **what went wrong**.
4. Ask what would have made the case easier.
5. Record the case as a short narrative, not just an opinion.

Example prompt:

> Think of a recent drainage complaint that took longer than it should have. Can you walk me through what happened, from the first phone call to the repair?

This produces concrete evidence about data flows, handovers, and failure points.

---

## 8. Do not ask

- Do not ask for individual owner names, tax amounts, arrears, or complaint details.
- Do not ask participants to criticize named colleagues.
- Do not ask for confidential security information.
- Do not ask leading questions such as “Don’t you think GIS would solve this?”
- Do not promise that the system will be built or that their department will receive funding.

---

## 9. Closing

> Thank you. Is there anything you expected me to ask that I did not? May I contact you if I need to clarify a point? Would you be willing to share a blank or redacted example of the form or register we discussed?

After the interview:

- Complete the response template within 24 hours.
- Write a one-paragraph reflection on the setting, tone, and any non-verbal observations.
- Note any documents promised.
- Assign a participant code and store the file securely.

---

## 9A. GIScience probes for Transactions in GIS

Because the target journal is *Transactions in GIS*, add these probes where relevant:

- What coordinate system, datum, or map projection do you use?
- How are field names, codes, and categories standardized?
- How is metadata recorded (source, date, accuracy, owner)?
- How are duplicate or overlapping features handled?
- How are linear assets (roads, drains, pipes) connected at nodes?
- How are addresses and parcels linked?
- What happens when a boundary or asset changes?
- How are data exported or shared with other systems?
- What data quality checks are performed?
- What would make the data interoperable with WASA, LGED, BBS, or national NSDI datasets?

These questions connect the local government workflow to the GIScience contribution.
