# Coding Frame and Analysis Template

This frame is a starting point. Revise it after the first three interviews. The goal is to move from individual responses to patterns that answer the research questions.

---

## 1. Analytic approach

Use reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006):

1. Familiarise yourself with the notes and transcripts.
2. Generate initial codes.
3. Collate codes into candidate themes.
4. Review themes against the data.
5. Define and name themes.
6. Write the analytic narrative with evidence.

Keep an audit trail:

- codebook version;
- date;
- coder;
- examples;
- changes to the frame;
- analytic memos.

## 3. Inductive codes

Add codes that emerge from the data. Examples:

- `LANDLORD_TENANT` — property records and occupancy mismatch.
- `WARD_KNOWLEDGE` — councillor or field officer as the real address index.
- `SEASONALITY` — monsoon, flooding, or heat shaping data needs.
- `PHONE_PHOTO` — WhatsApp/phone photos as informal evidence.
- `FEAR_OF_MONITORING` — staff worry that data will be used against them.
- `DUPLICATE_PROJECT` — overlapping data collection by projects.
- `LANGUAGE` — Bangla/English naming and search issues.
- `TENDER_DATA` — contractor data quality and handover.

## 5. Evidence log

For each theme, keep a table of evidence:

| Theme | Participant | Quote or observation | Interpretation | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | High / medium / low |

Confidence should reflect:

- how many participants said it;
- whether it is supported by documents;
- whether it is a personal opinion or an operational fact;
- whether it contradicts other evidence.

---

## 6. Negative cases

Record evidence that contradicts the emerging argument. Examples:

- a department with a good register;
- a successful data-sharing arrangement;
- a public map that works;
- a paper process that is faster than the digital one.

Negative cases strengthen the analysis and prevent overgeneralisation.

---

## 7. Analytic memos

Write a short memo after each interview:

- What did I learn?
- What surprised me?
- What confirms or challenges the design?
- What should I ask next?
- What document should I request?
- What quote should I keep?

## 9. Saturation

Stop adding participants when:

- the same themes recur across departments;
- no new codes are emerging;
- new participants confirm rather than challenge the pattern;
- the remaining gaps are about documents, not people.

A sample of 10–15 is usually sufficient for a focused institutional case study. Record the saturation decision in a memo.
