# Illustrative Municipal Geoportal Cases

These cases are used as **illustrations of mature municipal spatial data practice**, not as direct templates for RCC. Access dates should be updated at submission.

## Australia

### City of Perth — Perth GeoHub
- URL: https://geohub-perth.opendata.arcgis.com/
- Also: https://perth.wa.gov.au/building-and-planning/planning-and-building-applications/mapping
- Public interface includes property, planning, security cameras, transport, events, and 3D Perth views.
- Useful lesson: a single address/property entry point can connect planning, services, and public information.

### Wollongong City Council
- URL: https://www.wollongong.nsw.gov.au/
- Council provides online maps and property information, including land zone and planning information.
- Useful lesson: property and planning information are treated as routine public services, not specialist GIS outputs.

### Camden Council
- URL: https://www.camden.nsw.gov.au/media-centre/latest-news/click-into-camden-from-a-new-perspective-discover-the-layers-of-your-suburb-with-camdens-interactive-mapping-tool
- Interactive mapping covers general land information, flood, heritage, and Section 7.11 contributions.
- Useful lesson: a public map can combine statutory planning information with local context.

### Wodonga Council
- URL: https://maps.wodonga.vic.gov.au/
- Open data hub: https://cow-open-data-hub-cityofwodonga.hub.arcgis.com/
- Public GIS includes property, school crossings, parking, and open datasets.
- Useful lesson: a small council can publish useful operational layers without a large GIS team, provided the data and governance are in place.

## Other international references

- City of London / London Datastore — open data and urban analytics.
- Amsterdam — open data and smart-city governance.
- New York City — open data, MapPLUTO, and municipal service maps.
- Singapore OneMap — national address and property search.
- Indian urban local bodies — GIS in e-governance and property tax (Mukherjee, 2018, 2020; Singh et al., 2022).
- Tanzania secondary cities — GIS for revenue mobilisation (Namangaya, 2018).

## What to copy and what not to copy

**Copy:**
- address/property search as the front door;
- grouped layers by citizen need;
- metadata and data-date statements;
- print and mobile access;
- links to council services;
- selective open data;
- clear custodianship.

**Do not copy:**
- the full layer list;
- high-cost proprietary stacks;
- assumptions about address quality;
- assumptions about staff capacity;
- publication without governance.

## Citation note

For the article, cite the official council pages as grey literature with access dates, and use Jacoby et al. (2002) for the Australian state–local SDI context. Do not imply that these councils use the same software stack as RCC.
